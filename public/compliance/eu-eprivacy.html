ePrivacy Directive

Directive 2002/58/EC · in force since 2002-07-31 · mandatory-for-scope · official text (free)

Applies to: Providers of publicly available electronic communications services (Art. 4) and anyone storing or reading information on user terminals (Art. 5(3)).

Art. 1 Scope and aim
Art. 1(1) Aligns national rules so privacy rights are equally protected in electronic communications, allowing free data movement. Coming soon
Art. 1(2) Supplements the earlier data protection directive and covers legitimate interests of corporate subscribers. Coming soon
Art. 1(3) Excludes state security, defence, public security and criminal law activities from the directive. Coming soon
Art. 2 DefinitionsEarlier directives' definitions apply, plus extra terms listed below.
Art. 2(a) Defines a user as any individual using a public communications service, subscribed or not. Coming soon
Art. 2(b) Defines traffic data as data handled to carry or bill a communication. Coming soon
Art. 2(c) Defines location data as data revealing a user's device geographic position. Coming soon
Art. 2(d) Defines a communication as information exchanged between a limited set of parties; broadcasting is excluded. Coming soon
Art. 2(f) User or subscriber consent has the same meaning as data subject consent. Coming soon
Art. 2(g) Defines value added service as one needing traffic or location data beyond transmission or billing. Coming soon
Art. 2(h) Defines electronic mail as messages over public networks stored until the recipient collects them. Coming soon
Art. 2(i) Defines a personal data breach as a security failure destroying, losing, altering or exposing personal data. Coming soon
Art. 3 Services concernedDirective covers personal data processing for public electronic communications services, including data collection and identification devices. Coming soon
Art. 4 ►M2 Security of processing ◄
Art. 4(1) Providers must apply technical and organisational safeguards proportionate to risk for service and network security. Coming soon
Art. 4(1a) Security measures must limit access to authorised staff, guard data against loss or exposure, and follow a policy. We detect

arch.privileged-key-client-sideA privileged key in browser code lets any user reach subscriber data, so access is not limited to authorised personnel as Art. 4(1a) requires.

db.rls-missingA table without row-level security lets every API role read all rows, so stored personal data is not protected against unauthorised access.

secrets.committedA credential readable by everyone with the repository opens the personal data it guards to unauthorised people.

secrets.in-historyA secret left in git history and not rotated keeps the data it guards open to anyone with a clone.

tls.verification-disabledWithout certificate checks an interceptor can read or alter personal data in transit, against the protection of transmitted data Art. 4(1a) requires.
Art. 4(2) Providers must warn subscribers of particular network security risks and any remedies outside their control. Coming soon
Art. 4(3) Providers must notify authorities, and affected people where harmful, of personal data breaches without delay. Coming soon
Art. 4(4) Authorities may issue breach-notification guidance and audit; providers keep an inventory of breaches. Coming soon
Art. 4(5) Commission may adopt technical implementing measures for notification circumstances, format and procedures after consultation. Coming soon
Art. 5 Confidentiality of the communications
Art. 5(1) States must guarantee communications confidentiality and ban interception or surveillance without user consent or legal authority. Coming soon
Art. 5(2) Lawful business recording of communications as evidence of transactions remains permitted. Coming soon
Art. 5(3) Storing or reading information on a user's device requires informed consent, unless strictly necessary for the requested service. Coming soon
Art. 6 Traffic data
Art. 6(1) Traffic data must be erased or anonymised once no longer needed for transmission. Coming soon
Art. 6(2) Traffic data needed for billing and interconnection may be processed until payment can no longer be challenged. Coming soon
Art. 6(3) Marketing or value added use of traffic data needs prior consent, which can be withdrawn. Coming soon
Art. 6(4) Providers must tell users which traffic data is processed and for how long. Coming soon
Art. 6(5) Traffic data handling is limited to authorised staff and to what the activity needs. Coming soon
Art. 6(6) Bodies may still obtain traffic data to settle billing or interconnection disputes. Coming soon
Art. 7 Itemised billing
Art. 7(1) Subscribers may choose bills without itemised call detail. Coming soon
Art. 7(2) States must balance itemised billing against caller privacy, such as through alternative private communication or payment. Coming soon
Art. 8 Presentation and restriction of calling and connected line identification
Art. 8(1) Callers must be able to block their number display per call and per line, free. Coming soon
Art. 8(2) Called subscribers must be able to block display of incoming callers' numbers, simply and free. Coming soon
Art. 8(3) Called subscribers must be able to reject calls whose caller number is withheld. Coming soon
Art. 8(4) Called subscribers must be able to stop their connected line identity being shown to callers. Coming soon
Art. 8(5) Line identification rules extend to calls to and from third countries. Coming soon
Art. 8(6) Providers must inform the public about line identification options when offered. Coming soon
Art. 9 Location data other than traffic data
Art. 9(1) Location data beyond traffic data needs anonymisation or informed, withdrawable consent for value added services. Coming soon
Art. 9(2) Even after consent, users can temporarily refuse location processing simply and free of charge. Coming soon
Art. 9(3) Location data processing is limited to authorised persons and to what the service needs. Coming soon
Art. 10 ExceptionsStates must set transparent procedures for providers overriding line identification blocking or location consent refusal.
Art. 10(a) Caller identification blocking may be overridden temporarily to trace malicious or nuisance calls. Coming soon
Art. 10(b) Emergency organisations may override caller ID blocking and location refusal to answer emergency calls. Coming soon
Art. 11 Automatic call forwardingSubscribers must be able to stop third-party automatic call forwarding to their terminal, simply and free. Coming soon
Art. 12 Directories of subscribers
Art. 12(1) Subscribers must be told, free, about directory purposes before being listed. Coming soon
Art. 12(2) Subscribers choose whether and which data appear in directories, and may correct or withdraw it, free. Coming soon
Art. 12(3) States may require extra consent for directory uses beyond name-based contact lookup. Coming soon
Art. 12(4) Directory rights apply to individuals; states must also protect other subscribers' legitimate interests. Coming soon
Art. 13 Unsolicited communications
Art. 13(1) Automated calls, fax or email marketing is allowed only with prior consent. Coming soon
Art. 13(2) Existing customers' email may be used for similar own-product marketing if opt-out is offered each time. Coming soon
Art. 13(3) States must ensure other unsolicited marketing needs consent or respects refusals, at no charge. Coming soon
Art. 13(4) Marketing email hiding the sender's identity or lacking a valid opt-out address is prohibited. Coming soon
Art. 13(5) Consent rules apply to individual subscribers; states protect organisations' legitimate interests too. Coming soon
Art. 13(6) Affected persons and providers may sue over breaches of the marketing rules; states may set penalties. Coming soon
Art. 14 Technical features and standardisation
Art. 14(1) States must not impose terminal equipment technical requirements that hinder market circulation. Coming soon
Art. 14(2) States must notify the Commission when network technical features are required to implement the directive. Coming soon
Art. 14(3) Measures may require terminal equipment to let users protect and control their personal data. Coming soon
Art. 14a Committee procedure
Art. 14a(1) A communications committee assists the Commission. Coming soon
Art. 14a(2) Specifies which committee decision procedure provisions apply for the scrutiny procedure. Coming soon
Art. 14a(3) Specifies a variant set of committee procedure provisions applying where referenced. Coming soon
Art. 15 Application of certain provisions of Directive 95/46/EC
Art. 15(1) States may restrict rights for security, defence, crime prevention, including limited-period data retention, if proportionate. Coming soon
Art. 15(1a) Restriction power does not cover data mandated for retention by the data retention directive. Coming soon
Art. 15(1b) Providers must have internal procedures for handling access requests and report statistics to authorities. Coming soon
Art. 15(2) Judicial remedies, liability and sanctions of the data protection directive apply here. Coming soon
Art. 15(3) The Article 29 working party also covers matters under this directive. Coming soon
Art. 15a Implementation and enforcement
Art. 15a(1) States must set effective, proportionate, dissuasive penalties and notify them to the Commission. Coming soon
Art. 15a(2) National authorities must be able to order infringements to stop. Coming soon
Art. 15a(3) Authorities need investigative powers and resources, including obtaining relevant information. Coming soon
Art. 15a(4) Regulators may adopt cross-border cooperation measures, informing the Commission beforehand. Coming soon
Art. 16 Transitional arrangements
Art. 16(1) Directory rules do not apply to editions already produced before national rules took effect. Coming soon
Art. 16(2) Existing directory entries may stay unless subscribers object after receiving full information. Coming soon
Art. 17 Transposition
Art. 17(1) States had to adopt compliant national provisions by October 2003 and inform the Commission. Coming soon
Art. 17(2) States must send the Commission their national law texts and later amendments. Coming soon
Art. 18 ReviewCommission must report on the directive's application and may propose amendments. Coming soon
Art. 19 RepealRepeals the earlier telecommunications privacy directive, with references redirected here. Coming soon
