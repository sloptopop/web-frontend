Critical Entities Resilience Directive

Directive (EU) 2022/2557 · in force since 2023-01-16 · mandatory-for-scope · official text (free)

Applies to: Critical entities designated by member states (physical resilience).

Art. 1 Subject matter and scope
Art. 1(1) Opens the list of what the Directive does.
Art. 1(1)(a) Member States must identify critical entities and support them so essential services run unobstructed across the market. Coming soon
Art. 1(1)(b) Sets duties on critical entities to strengthen resilience and keep essential services running. Coming soon
Art. 1(1)(c) Introduces the rules the Directive establishes.
Art. 1(1)(c)(i) Establishes rules for supervising critical entities. Coming soon
Art. 1(1)(c)(ii) Establishes rules for enforcement. Coming soon
Art. 1(1)(c)(iii) Rules for designating entities of particular European significance and advisory missions assessing their measures. Coming soon
Art. 1(1)(d) Creates shared procedures for cooperation and for reporting on how the Directive is applied. Coming soon
Art. 1(1)(e) Sets measures aiming at high resilience of critical entities and a better-functioning internal market. Coming soon
Art. 1(2) Directive excludes matters under the NIS2 Directive; Member States must coordinate implementing both. Coming soon
Art. 1(3) Equivalent sector-specific EU resilience rules displace corresponding provisions of this Directive, including supervision. Coming soon
Art. 1(4) Confidential information is shared with authorities only when necessary, limited to what is proportionate, and kept confidential. Coming soon
Art. 1(5) Member States keep responsibility for national security, defence and essential state functions. Coming soon
Art. 1(6) Directive excludes public administration bodies working in security, defence or law enforcement. Coming soon
Art. 1(7) States may exempt security-related critical entities from certain articles and chapters. Coming soon
Art. 1(8) No obligation to supply information whose disclosure would harm national security or defence interests. Coming soon
Art. 1(9) Directive leaves EU personal data protection law untouched. Coming soon
Art. 2 DefinitionsIntroduces the definitions used in the Directive.
Art. 2(1) Defines a critical entity as one a Member State has designated in an Annex category. Coming soon
Art. 2(2) Defines resilience as an entity's capacity to withstand, handle and recover from incidents. Coming soon
Art. 2(3) Defines an incident as an event that could or does disrupt an essential service. Coming soon
Art. 2(4) Defines critical infrastructure as assets and systems needed to provide an essential service. Coming soon
Art. 2(5) Defines an essential service as one vital to society, economy, health, safety or environment. Coming soon
Art. 2(6) Defines risk as potential loss or disruption, combining magnitude and likelihood. Coming soon
Art. 2(7) Defines risk assessment as the process of analysing threats, vulnerabilities and potential disruption. Coming soon
Art. 2(8) Standard has the meaning given in the EU standardisation regulation. Coming soon
Art. 2(9) Technical specification has the meaning given in the EU standardisation regulation. Coming soon
Art. 2(10) Defines public administration entity, excluding judiciary, parliaments and central banks, subject to listed criteria.
Art. 2(10)(a) Public administration entity criterion: created for general-interest needs, not commercial. Coming soon
Art. 2(10)(b) Public administration entity criterion: has legal personality or may act for one that does. Coming soon
Art. 2(10)(c) Public administration entity criterion: mainly state-financed or supervised, or board majority state-appointed. Coming soon
Art. 2(10)(d) Public administration entity criterion: can issue binding decisions affecting cross-border movement rights. Coming soon
Art. 3 Minimum harmonisationMember States may keep or adopt stricter national resilience rules consistent with EU law. Coming soon
Art. 4 Strategy on the resilience of critical entities
Art. 4(1) Each Member State must adopt a national resilience strategy by January 2026 after consultation. Coming soon
Art. 4(2) Strategy must contain listed elements and be updated at least every four years.
Art. 4(2)(a) Strategy must state objectives and priorities, considering cross-border and cross-sector dependencies. Coming soon
Art. 4(2)(b) Strategy must set a governance framework describing roles of authorities and entities. Coming soon
Art. 4(2)(c) Strategy must describe resilience-enhancing measures, including the national risk assessment. Coming soon
Art. 4(2)(d) Strategy must describe how critical entities are identified. Coming soon
Art. 4(2)(e) Strategy must describe support processes and public-private cooperation measures. Coming soon
Art. 4(2)(f) Strategy must list main authorities and stakeholders involved besides critical entities. Coming soon
Art. 4(2)(g) Strategy must set a framework for coordinating with cybersecurity authorities on risk information and supervision. Coming soon
Art. 4(2)(h) Strategy must describe measures helping small and medium critical entities meet Chapter III duties. Coming soon
Art. 4(3) Strategies and major updates go to the Commission within three months. Coming soon
Art. 5 Risk assessment by Member States
Art. 5(1) Member States must run national risk assessments by January 2026 and every four years, using essential services list. Coming soon
Art. 5(2) National risk assessments must consider listed inputs and involve cooperation with other countries.
Art. 5(2)(a) National assessment must consider the general EU civil protection risk assessment. Coming soon
Art. 5(2)(b) National assessment must consider risk assessments required by sector-specific EU legislation. Coming soon
Art. 5(2)(c) National assessment must consider interdependencies between sectors and countries and effects of disruption. Coming soon
Art. 5(2)(d) National assessment must use information from incidents notified under Article 15. Coming soon
Art. 5(3) States must give critical entities relevant parts of national risk assessments to support their own assessments. Coming soon
Art. 5(4) Within three months of an assessment, States give the Commission information on identified risks per sector. Coming soon
Art. 5(5) Commission develops an optional shared template for reporting risk assessment results. Coming soon
Art. 6 Identification of critical entities
Art. 6(1) Member States must identify critical entities in Annex sectors by July 2026. Coming soon
Art. 6(2) Identification must use the national risk assessment, strategy and three cumulative criteria.
Art. 6(2)(a) Criterion: the entity provides at least one essential service. Coming soon
Art. 6(2)(b) Criterion: entity operates and has critical infrastructure in that Member State. Coming soon
Art. 6(2)(c) Criterion: an incident would significantly disrupt its services or dependent services. Coming soon
Art. 6(3) States keep a list of critical entities, notify them within a month, and state their obligations. Coming soon
Art. 6(4) Authorities must tell cybersecurity authorities which critical entities they identified, within a month. Coming soon
Art. 6(5) List of critical entities is reviewed at least every four years; removed entities are notified. Coming soon
Art. 6(6) Commission develops non-binding guidance helping States identify critical entities. Coming soon
Art. 7 Significant disruptive effect
Art. 7(1) States must weigh listed criteria when judging whether a disruptive effect is significant.
Art. 7(1)(a) Disruption significance factor: how many users depend on the service. Coming soon
Art. 7(1)(b) Disruption significance factor: how much other sectors depend on the service. Coming soon
Art. 7(1)(c) Disruption significance factor: degree and duration of impact on society, environment, safety and health. Coming soon
Art. 7(1)(d) Disruption significance factor: the entity's market share. Coming soon
Art. 7(1)(e) Disruption significance factor: geographic area affected, including cross-border and isolated regions. Coming soon
Art. 7(1)(f) Disruption significance factor: entity's importance given availability of alternatives. Coming soon
Art. 7(2) After identification, States send the Commission specified information and refresh it at least every four years.
Art. 7(2)(a) Report any additional essential services beyond the Commission's list. Coming soon
Art. 7(2)(b) Report number of critical entities per sector, subsector and essential service. Coming soon
Art. 7(2)(c) Report thresholds used to specify the significance criteria. Coming soon
Art. 7(3) Commission issues non-binding guidelines on applying the disruption criteria after consulting the Resilience Group. Coming soon

Art. 9(1) Each Member State designates competent authorities to apply and enforce the Directive. Coming soon
Art. 9(2) Each Member State designates a single point of contact for cross-border liaison. Coming soon

Art. 10(1) Member States support critical entities' resilience through guidance, exercises, training or possibly funding. Coming soon

Art. 11 Cooperation between Member States
Art. 11(1) Member States consult each other on critical entities to ensure consistent application.
Art. 11(1)(a) Consultation case: entities using infrastructure physically linking several Member States. Coming soon
Art. 11(1)(b) Consultation case: entities in corporate structures tied to entities in other States. Coming soon

Art. 12 Risk assessment by critical entities
Art. 12(1) Critical entities must assess risks within nine months and at least every four years. Coming soon
Art. 12(2) Entity risk assessments must cover all natural and man-made risks and sector dependencies. Coming soon
Art. 13 Resilience measures of critical entities
Art. 13(1) Critical entities must take appropriate technical, security and organisational resilience measures based on risk assessments.
Art. 13(1)(a) Measures to prevent incidents, considering disaster risk reduction and climate adaptation. Coming soon
Art. 13(1)(b) Adequate physical protection of premises and infrastructure, such as fencing, monitoring and access control. Coming soon
Art. 13(1)(c) Measures to respond to and resist incidents, with crisis procedures and alert routines. Coming soon
Art. 13(1)(d) Measures to recover from incidents, including business continuity and alternative supply chains. Coming soon
Art. 13(1)(e) Employee security management: critical personnel categories, access rights, background checks and training. Coming soon
Art. 13(1)(f) Raise personnel awareness of the resilience measures through training, materials and exercises. Coming soon
Art. 13(2) Critical entities must have and apply a resilience plan documenting their measures. Coming soon
Art. 13(3) Each critical entity names a liaison officer as contact for authorities. Coming soon
Art. 13(4) Commission may organise advisory missions to help a consenting critical entity meet its duties. Coming soon
Art. 13(5) Commission adopts non-binding guidelines detailing resilience measures after consulting the Resilience Group. Coming soon

Art. 14 Background checks
Art. 14(1) States define when critical entities may request background checks on certain persons.
Art. 14(1)(a) Background checks may cover people holding sensitive roles relating to resilience. Coming soon
Art. 14(1)(b) Background checks may cover people with physical or remote access to premises or systems. Coming soon
Art. 14(1)(c) Background checks may cover candidates for those sensitive or access positions. Coming soon
Art. 14(2) Background check requests are processed lawfully, proportionately and only to assess security risk. Coming soon
Art. 14(3) Checks must meet minimum content and use the European criminal records system.
Art. 14(3)(a) Background check must confirm the person's identity. Coming soon
Art. 14(3)(b) Background check must examine criminal records for offences relevant to the position. Coming soon
Art. 15 Incident notification
Art. 15(1) Critical entities report significant incidents promptly: initial notice within 24 hours, detailed report within a month.
Art. 15(1)(a) Significance parameter: number and share of users affected. Coming soon
Art. 15(1)(b) Significance parameter: how long the disruption lasts. Coming soon
Art. 15(1)(c) Significance parameter: geographic area affected, considering isolation. Coming soon
Art. 15(2) Notifications include available information on nature, cause, consequences and cross-border impact, without added liability. Coming soon
Art. 15(3) Authorities relay incident information through contact points to affected States, protecting confidentiality. Coming soon
Art. 15(4) Authority must promptly give the notifying entity follow-up information; public may be informed if in public interest. Coming soon
Art. 16 StandardsMember States should encourage European and international standards for resilience measures, without favouring particular technology. Coming soon

Art. 17(1) Defines when an entity counts as critical of particular European significance.
Art. 17(1)(a) Entity must already be identified as a critical entity. Coming soon
Art. 17(1)(b) Entity provides same or similar essential services in six or more Member States. Coming soon

Art. 21(1) Member States give authorities powers to assess compliance of critical entities.
Art. 21(1)(a) Authorities can inspect premises on site and supervise measures off site. Coming soon
Art. 21(1)(b) Authorities can conduct or order audits of critical entities. Coming soon
