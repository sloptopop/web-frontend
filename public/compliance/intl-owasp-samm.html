OWASP Software Assurance Maturity Model

OWASP SAMM v2, business functions, security practices, streams and maturity-level activities · current · voluntary · official text (free)

Applies to: Organisations assessing or improving their software assurance practices; contractual when a customer requires a SAMM level.

  • 90 points
  • 0 we fix
  • 12 we detect
  • 78 coming soon
We fix
sloptopop finds evidence of a breach of this point in your code, and at least one of those checks has an automatic fix that has passed review on real code.
We detect
sloptopop finds evidence of a breach of this point in your code, and you fix it.

G-SM Strategy & Metrics
G-SM-A Create and Promote
G-SM-A-1 Gather executive risk tolerance and threats, document drivers, and publish a baseline for risk profiling. Coming soon
G-SM-A-2 Create and publish a one-to-three-year application security roadmap with milestones and budget. Coming soon
G-SM-A-3 Review the security plan at least yearly and track roadmap milestone success with dashboards. Coming soon
G-SM-B Measure and Improve
G-SM-B-1 Define effort, result and environment metrics to gauge the security program's effectiveness. Coming soon
G-SM-B-2 Set documented, stable KPIs from collected metrics and share them with teams and leadership. Coming soon
G-SM-B-3 Use KPIs and metrics to guide ongoing improvement of the security program. Coming soon
G-PC Policy & Compliance
G-PC-A Policy and Standards
G-PC-A-1 Build a library of security policies and technology-specific standards, reviewed with product teams. Coming soon
G-PC-A-2 Provide versioned security requirements and test scripts linked to policies for product teams. Coming soon
G-PC-A-3 Measure each application's compliance with policies and standards, reporting and escalating gaps. Coming soon
G-PC-B Compliance Management
G-PC-B-1 List external compliance obligations, determine which applications are in scope, and map to internal standards. Coming soon
G-PC-B-2 Maintain regulatory requirements with test scripts, and reassess each application's compliance periodically. Coming soon
G-PC-B-3 Measure and report compliance status, detect regressions via automation, and track remediation. Coming soon
G-EG Education & Guidance
G-EG-A Training and Awareness
G-EG-A-1 Give all staff involved in software mandatory basic security awareness training with sign-off. Coming soon
G-EG-A-2 Provide security training tailored to each role and technology, starting with developers. Coming soon
G-EG-A-3 Run a formal onboarding training program, verified by testing, before staff work on critical tasks. Coming soon
G-EG-B Organization and Culture
G-EG-B-1 Appoint a Security Champion in each development team as liaison to security. Coming soon
G-EG-B-2 Establish a chartered center of excellence that defines secure development practices and supports product champions. Coming soon
G-EG-B-3 Provide communication platforms so teams share security knowledge and communities form around technologies. Coming soon
D-TA Threat Assessment
D-TA-A Application Risk Profile
D-TA-A-1 Classify each application by business risk using a short questionnaire and simple scale. Coming soon
D-TA-A-2 Evaluate risk, privacy and cross-application impact for all applications and keep a central inventory. Coming soon
D-TA-A-3 Review application risk profiles periodically and re-evaluate when conditions change. Coming soon
D-TA-B Threat Modeling
D-TA-B-1 Perform ad-hoc, lightweight threat modeling for high-risk applications and persist the results. We detect

arch.threat-model-missingLevel 1 threat modelling persists its outcome; a network-facing repository with no threat model shows none was kept.
D-TA-B-2 Adopt a standardized threat modeling method with diagrams, threat identification, mitigations and update triggers. Coming soon
D-TA-B-3 Integrate threat modeling into the SDLC, reuse risk patterns, and keep improving the method. Coming soon
D-SR Security Requirements
D-SR-A Software Requirements
D-SR-A-1 Review functional requirements to derive specific, measurable security objectives, including misuse perspectives. Coming soon
D-SR-A-2 Elicit security requirements from varied sources and specify them alongside functional requirements with enforcement. Coming soon
D-SR-A-3 Provide a framework with reusable categorised security requirements and quality guidance for project teams. Coming soon
D-SR-B Supplier Security
D-SR-B-1 Assess suppliers' security practices through checklists or interviews and state expectations explicitly. Coming soon
D-SR-B-2 Contractually define supplier security responsibilities, service levels and liabilities, working toward a standard agreement. Coming soon
D-SR-B-3 Align supplier development processes and tools with yours, adding compensating controls where they fall short. Coming soon
D-SA Secure Architecture
D-SA-A Architecture Design
D-SA-A-1 Product teams apply a short checklist of security design principles to perimeter interfaces. Coming soon
D-SA-A-2 Identify, standardise and document shared security services so product teams can integrate them consistently. Coming soon
D-SA-A-3 Build and maintain reference architectures of verified security components, and monitor gaps in them. Coming soon
D-SA-B Technology Management
D-SA-B-1 Identify key technologies and integrations per application and evaluate their security risks. Coming soon
D-SA-B-2 Publish a reviewed list of recommended technologies, chosen on security track record, as defaults. Coming soon
D-SA-B-3 Enforce and monitor use of standardised technologies, and detect non-recommended ones. Coming soon
I-SB Secure Build
I-SB-A Build Process
I-SB-A-1 Define the build process centrally and repeatably, free of secrets, with hardened tools and artifact integrity values. We detect

toolchain.unpinnedA floating toolchain means the defined build cannot be followed to produce the same result each time.
I-SB-A-2 Automate builds, protect build tooling and signing credentials, sign artifacts, and add automated security checks to the pipeline. We detect

ci.missingNo CI workflow runs the build and checks, so the build process is not automated as level 2 requires.
I-SB-A-3 Define pass criteria for security checks in builds, fail builds on violations, and centralise signing. We detect

ci.nonblocking-checksA check that cannot fail the build does not enforce the baseline that level 3 requires builds to meet.
I-SB-B Software Dependencies
I-SB-B-1 Keep a record of all dependencies with usage, version, licence, source and support status, and check for vulnerabilities. We detect

lockfile.missingWithout a lockfile there is no record of the exact dependency versions in use, the bill of materials level 1 asks for.
I-SB-B-2 Maintain approved dependency lists and a central repository, review dependencies regularly, and scan for vulnerabilities. Coming soon
I-SB-B-3 Whitelist approved dependencies and versions, fail builds on others, verify them like own code, and track issues. Coming soon
I-SD Secure Deployment
I-SD-A Deployment Process
I-SD-A-1 Define the deployment process end-to-end, centrally stored, with hardened tools and no developer production access. Coming soon
I-SD-A-2 Automate deployment across stages, integrate security scans, stop on severe issues, and audit each deployment. Coming soon
I-SD-A-3 Verify signatures of artifacts before deployment and refuse to deploy unverifiable or expired-certificate ones. Coming soon
I-SD-B Secret Management
I-SD-B-1 Restrict developer access to production secrets, encrypt them, and keep them out of repositories and non-production configs. We detect

arch.privileged-key-client-sideA privileged key reachable from every browser is the opposite of limiting secret access by least privilege.

secrets.committedSecrets are kept unprotected in files stored in the code repository.
I-SD-B-2 Inject secrets automatically at deploy time, scan repositories including history for secrets, and protect secret storage. We detect

secrets.committedSource files still contain active secrets, which level 2 says they no longer should.

secrets.in-historyThe secret is in repository history, which level 2 checks include; it must be refreshed on the consuming system.
I-SD-B-3 Manage secret lifecycle with per-instance secrets, centrally log all secret access, and review the logs. We detect

secrets.insecure-defaultA sample secret reused as the default means instances share one known value instead of generated per-instance secrets.
I-DM Defect Management
I-DM-A Defect Tracking
I-DM-A-1 Define security defects, record and track them in a known location with access rules and basic classification. Coming soon
I-DM-A-2 Rate security defects consistently, set fix SLAs, monitor breaches, and handle exceptions with stakeholder awareness. Coming soon
I-DM-A-3 Automate SLA-breach alerts, feed defects into risk management, and integrate defect data with build and monitoring. Coming soon
I-DM-B Metrics and Feedback
I-DM-B-1 Periodically extract basic metrics from security defects and act on quick wins. Coming soon
I-DM-B-2 Collect unified defect metrics organisation-wide, report regularly, and share lessons across teams. Coming soon
I-DM-B-3 Review defect metrics yearly, drop low-value ones, and use aggregated data to steer security strategy. Coming soon
V-AA Architecture Assessment
V-AA-A Architecture Validation
V-AA-A-1 Build a high-level architecture view and check general security mechanisms are properly provided. Coming soon
V-AA-A-2 Review each interface's architecture against security and compliance requirements, including data flows and design deviations. Coming soon
V-AA-A-3 Assess whether architecture security controls are effective, scalable and aligned with strategy, feeding findings to defect management. Coming soon
V-AA-B Architecture Mitigation
V-AA-B-1 Review the architecture for typical security threats and weaknesses in mechanisms or components. Coming soon
V-AA-B-2 Check every identified threat is mitigated by design, and link threats to countermeasures in the threat model. We detect

arch.threat-model-missingPoint requires linking threats to countermeasures in an existing threat model; finding shows a network-facing repository has no such model.
V-AA-B-3 Continuously improve enterprise architecture based on the outcome of architecture reviews. Coming soon
V-RT Requirements-driven Testing
V-RT-A Control Verification
V-RT-A-1 Run security tests confirming standard controls such as authentication, access control, validation and encryption work. Coming soon
V-RT-A-2 Derive security test cases from security requirements and report pass or fail results. Coming soon
V-RT-A-3 Automate regression and security unit tests for fixed bugs, and require them to pass before merge or build. Coming soon
V-RT-B Misuse/Abuse Testing
V-RT-B-1 Fuzz the main input parameters with malformed data to observe behaviour on unexpected input. Coming soon
V-RT-B-2 Create misuse and abuse cases and tests to detect business logic flaws. Coming soon
V-RT-B-3 Run denial-of-service and stress tests in controlled conditions to learn resilience and resource exhaustion limits. Coming soon
V-ST Security Testing
V-ST-A Scalable Baseline
V-ST-A-1 Use automated static and dynamic security testing tools, increasing frequency and coverage progressively. Coming soon
V-ST-A-2 Tune automated security tools to your stacks to cut false positives and negatives. Coming soon
V-ST-A-3 Run automated security tests within build and deploy, review findings promptly, and show results to stakeholders. We detect

ci.missingPoint requires security tests to execute automatically in the pipeline; finding shows no CI configuration runs the checks, so that automation is absent.
V-ST-B Deep Understanding
V-ST-B-1 Manually test high-risk components for vulnerabilities, guided by tools, and triage findings for remediation. Coming soon
V-ST-B-2 Run manual penetration tests against defined security cases before release; stakeholders review results and accept risks. Coming soon
V-ST-B-3 Run security testing alongside all development phases, feeding earlier findings into later tests and training. Coming soon
O-IM Incident Management
O-IM-A Incident Detection
O-IM-A-1 Review available logs to spot obvious security incidents, with backup coverage and defined contacts. Coming soon
O-IM-A-2 Run an owned, documented, largely automated detection process using log correlation, checklists and immediate out-of-hours notification. Coming soon
O-IM-A-3 Continuously improve detection using external, past-incident and threat-model inputs, with escalation when events go unacknowledged. Coming soon
O-IM-B Incident Response
O-IM-B-1 Name an incident response owner and contact, define participants, and record actions taken securely. Coming soon
O-IM-B-2 Document a formal incident response process with scenarios, triage, stakeholder involvement, root-cause analysis and trained responders. Coming soon
O-IM-B-3 Maintain a dedicated, always-available response team with exercises, automation, resources and metrics for continuous improvement. Coming soon
O-EM Environment Management
O-EM-A Configuration Hardening
O-EM-A-1 Apply secure configuration to technology stack components using available guidance and share learnings. We detect

tls.verification-disabledDisabling certificate checks is an insecure configuration of a key stack component instead of a hardened one.
O-EM-A-2 Establish owned, change-controlled hardening baselines and guides for every stack component, applied by teams. Coming soon
O-EM-A-3 Monitor deployed configurations against baselines, report results, and handle deviations as security defects. Coming soon
O-EM-B Patching and Updating
O-EM-B-1 Identify third-party components needing updates and know component versions, though patching may be ad hoc. Coming soon
O-EM-B-2 Follow a defined, scheduled patching process with prioritisation guidance and risk handling for unpatched critical flaws. Coming soon
O-EM-B-3 Track patch compliance with dashboards, support rapid component patching, and monitor external threat intelligence. Coming soon
O-OM Operational Management
O-OM-A Data Protection
O-OM-A-1 Understand data sensitivity and flow, and keep unsanitized production data out of lower environments. Coming soon
O-OM-A-2 Catalogue data by sensitivity and apply standardized protection across its lifetime, including backups. We detect

db.sensitive-column-plaintextA card, identity or health column without encryption shows no technical control protecting sensitive data at rest.
O-OM-A-3 Automate enforcement and monitoring of the data protection policy, and regularly review the catalogue and processes. Coming soon
O-OM-B System Decommissioning / Legacy Management
O-OM-B-1 Identify unused applications and product versions, route them to decommissioning, and end support for unused versions. Coming soon
O-OM-B-2 Follow a standard decommissioning process removing accounts, rules and data, and replace end-of-life dependencies promptly. Coming soon
O-OM-B-3 Track lifecycle and end-of-life of all assets, mitigate risks, and publish a support plan limiting active versions. Coming soon
