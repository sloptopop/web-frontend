Product Liability Directive (2024)

Directive (EU) 2024/2853 · in force since 2024-12-08; transposition period running · mandatory-for-scope · official text (free)

Applies to: Manufacturers of products, including software, through national transposition.

Art. 1 Subject matter and objectiveSets EU-wide liability rules for harm from faulty products, protecting consumers and the internal market. Coming soon
Art. 2 Scope
Art. 2(1) Rules cover products first sold or put into use after 9 December 2026. Coming soon
Art. 2(2) Open-source software built or provided outside any commercial activity is excluded. Coming soon
Art. 2(3) Nuclear accident damage is excluded where international conventions already cover liability. Coming soon
Art. 2(4) Introduces rights and rules that remain unaffected by this Directive.
Art. 2(4)(a) EU personal data protection law continues to apply alongside this Directive. Coming soon
Art. 2(4)(b) Injured people keep contract-based or other fault-based claims under national law. Coming soon
Art. 2(4)(c) Rights under special national liability systems existing in July 1985 are preserved. Coming soon
Art. 3 Level of harmonisationMember States may not adopt stricter or looser national rules than the Directive. Coming soon
Art. 4 DefinitionsIntroduces the defined terms used throughout the Directive.
Art. 4(1) Defines product broadly as movable goods, including software, electricity, raw materials and manufacturing files. Coming soon
Art. 4(2) Defines digital manufacturing files as digital templates that drive machines to make physical items. Coming soon
Art. 4(3) Defines related service as a digital service whose absence stops a product working. Coming soon
Art. 4(4) Defines component as any tangible or intangible item or service built into or linked with a product. Coming soon
Art. 4(5) Defines when a product remains under the manufacturer's control.
Art. 4(5)(a) Control exists when the manufacturer performs or approves integration or modification actions.
Art. 4(5)(a)(i) Covers manufacturer-approved integration, connection or supply of components, including software updates. Coming soon
Art. 4(5)(a)(ii) Covers manufacturer-approved modification of the product, including substantial changes. Coming soon
Art. 4(5)(b) Control also exists when the manufacturer can supply updates itself or through others. Coming soon
Art. 4(6) Data takes its meaning from the EU Data Governance Act. Coming soon
Art. 4(7) Defines making available as supplying a product on the EU market commercially, paid or free. Coming soon
Art. 4(8) Placing on the market means the first time a product is made available in the EU. Coming soon
Art. 4(9) Defines putting into service as first commercial use in the EU without prior market placement. Coming soon
Art. 4(10) Defines who counts as a manufacturer.
Art. 4(10)(a) A manufacturer includes anyone who develops, makes or produces a product. Coming soon
Art. 4(10)(b) Includes anyone commissioning a product or presenting themselves as maker via their brand. Coming soon
Art. 4(10)(c) Includes anyone who makes a product for their own use. Coming soon
Art. 4(11) Defines authorised representative as an EU-established party mandated in writing by a manufacturer. Coming soon
Art. 4(12) Defines importer as whoever places a non-EU product on the EU market. Coming soon
Art. 4(13) Defines fulfilment service provider as offering warehousing, packaging or dispatch without owning the goods. Coming soon
Art. 4(14) Defines distributor as a supply-chain party, other than maker or importer, supplying the product. Coming soon
Art. 4(15) Economic operator covers manufacturers, service providers, representatives, importers, fulfilment providers and distributors. Coming soon
Art. 4(16) Online platform takes its meaning from the Digital Services Act. Coming soon
Art. 4(17) Trade secret takes its meaning from the EU Trade Secrets Directive. Coming soon
Art. 4(18) Defines substantial modification as a post-market change to a product.
Art. 4(18)(a) A change is substantial if product safety rules deem it so. Coming soon
Art. 4(18)(b) Where safety rules set no threshold, a two-part test decides substantiality.
Art. 4(18)(b)(i) Change alters original performance, purpose or type beyond what the initial risk assessment foresaw. Coming soon
Art. 4(18)(b)(ii) Change alters the hazard's nature, creates a new hazard or raises risk. Coming soon
Art. 5 Right to compensation
Art. 5(1) Any natural person harmed by a defective product is entitled to compensation. Coming soon
Art. 5(2) Claims may also be brought by others on behalf of injured persons.
Art. 5(2)(a) Successors or subrogated parties may claim in the injured person's place. Coming soon
Art. 5(2)(b) Persons legally authorised to act for injured people may bring claims. Coming soon
Art. 6 Damage
Art. 6(1) Compensation is limited to specific categories of damage.
Art. 6(1)(a) Covers death and personal injury, including recognised psychological harm. Coming soon
Art. 6(1)(b) Covers property damage or destruction, subject to listed exceptions.
Art. 6(1)(b)(i) Damage to the defective product itself is not compensable. Coming soon
Art. 6(1)(b)(ii) Excludes a product damaged by a defective component the manufacturer integrated or controlled. Coming soon
Art. 6(1)(b)(iii) Property used solely for professional purposes is excluded. Coming soon
Art. 6(1)(c) Covers destruction or corruption of data not used professionally. Coming soon
Art. 6(2) Compensation covers all material losses and non-material losses where national law allows. Coming soon
Art. 6(3) National compensation rules under other liability regimes remain unaffected. Coming soon
Art. 7 Defectiveness
Art. 7(1) A product is defective if it lacks the safety people may expect or law requires. Coming soon
Art. 7(2) Defectiveness is judged by weighing all circumstances, with listed factors.
Art. 7(2)(a) Consider presentation and characteristics, including labelling, design, features and instructions. Coming soon
Art. 7(2)(b) Consider how the product might reasonably be expected to be used. Coming soon
Art. 7(2)(c) Consider effects of the product's ability to keep learning or gain features. Coming soon
Art. 7(2)(d) Consider foreseeable effects of other products used together or interconnected with it. Coming soon
Art. 7(2)(e) Consider when the product was placed on the market or left manufacturer control. Coming soon
Art. 7(2)(f) Consider relevant safety requirements, including safety-relevant cybersecurity requirements. Coming soon
Art. 7(2)(g) Consider any recall or safety intervention by authorities or economic operators. Coming soon
Art. 7(2)(h) Consider the specific needs of the intended user group. Coming soon
Art. 7(2)(i) For harm-preventing products, consider any failure to achieve that preventive purpose. Coming soon
Art. 7(3) A product is not defective merely because a better one or update appears later. Coming soon
Art. 8 Economic operators liable for defective products
Art. 8(1) Lists operators liable for damage; manufacturer liability extends to defective components under its control.
Art. 8(1)(a) The manufacturer of a defective product is liable. Coming soon
Art. 8(1)(b) A component manufacturer is liable when its component made a controlled product defective. Coming soon
Art. 8(1)(c) For non-EU manufacturers, other EU-based operators become liable as well.
Art. 8(1)(c)(i) The importer of the defective product or component is liable. Coming soon
Art. 8(1)(c)(ii) The manufacturer's authorised representative is liable. Coming soon
Art. 8(1)(c)(iii) Without importer or representative, the fulfilment service provider is liable. Coming soon
Art. 8(2) Anyone substantially modifying a product outside manufacturer control and supplying it is treated as manufacturer. Coming soon
Art. 8(3) Distributors become liable when no EU economic operator can be identified, under conditions.
Art. 8(3)(a) Condition: the injured person asks the distributor to name an operator or its supplier. Coming soon
Art. 8(3)(b) Condition: the distributor fails to name one within a month of the request. Coming soon
Art. 8(4) Online platform providers face the same distributor-style liability when certain conditions hold. Coming soon
Art. 8(5) States may use compensation schemes when victims cannot obtain redress from liable parties. Coming soon
Art. 9 Disclosure of evidence
Art. 9(1) Courts can order defendants to disclose relevant evidence when a claim is plausible. Coming soon
Art. 9(2) Defendants may likewise obtain relevant evidence from claimants when needed to counter claims. Coming soon
Art. 9(3) Evidence disclosure must be limited to what is necessary and proportionate. Coming soon
Art. 9(4) Courts weigh all parties' legitimate interests, including confidentiality and trade secrets, when ordering disclosure. Coming soon
Art. 9(5) Courts may take measures to protect trade secrets disclosed during or after proceedings. Coming soon
Art. 9(6) Courts may require disclosed evidence to be presented accessibly and understandably, if proportionate. Coming soon
Art. 9(7) National pre-trial disclosure rules, where they exist, are not affected. Coming soon
Art. 10 Burden of proof
Art. 10(1) Claimants must prove defect, damage and the causal link between them. Coming soon
Art. 10(2) Defectiveness is presumed in listed situations.
Art. 10(2)(a) Presumed where the defendant fails to disclose ordered relevant evidence. Coming soon
Art. 10(2)(b) Presumed where the product breaches mandatory safety rules aimed at the harm suffered. Coming soon
Art. 10(2)(c) Presumed where damage resulted from an obvious malfunction during normal foreseeable use. Coming soon
Art. 10(3) Causation is presumed once defect is established and damage fits that defect type. Coming soon
Art. 10(4) Courts presume defect or causation when proof is excessively hard despite disclosure.
Art. 10(4)(a) Requires claimant to face excessive proof difficulties, such as technical or scientific complexity. Coming soon
Art. 10(4)(b) Requires claimant to show defect or causation is likely. Coming soon
Art. 10(5) Defendants may rebut any of the presumptions. Coming soon
Art. 11 Exemption from liability
Art. 11(1) Economic operators escape liability if they prove one of the listed grounds.
Art. 11(1)(a) Manufacturer or importer proves it never placed the product on the market or in service. Coming soon
Art. 11(1)(b) Distributor proves it never made the product available. Coming soon
Art. 11(1)(c) Defect probably did not exist at market placement, or arose afterwards. Coming soon
Art. 11(1)(d) Defect results from the product complying with legal requirements. Coming soon
Art. 11(1)(e) Scientific and technical knowledge then available could not have revealed the defect. Coming soon
Art. 11(1)(f) Component maker shows defect stems from the final product's design or manufacturer's instructions. Coming soon
Art. 11(1)(g) Modifier shows the defect lies in a part the modification did not touch. Coming soon
Art. 11(2) Later-arising defects still bring liability when caused by controlled elements listed below.
Art. 11(2)(a) No exemption where a controlled related service causes the defect. Coming soon
Art. 11(2)(b) No exemption where controlled software, including updates or upgrades, causes the defect. Coming soon
Art. 11(2)(c) No exemption where missing software updates needed for safety cause the defect. Coming soon
Art. 11(2)(d) No exemption where a substantial modification causes the defect. Coming soon
Art. 12 Liability of multiple economic operators
Art. 12(1) Multiple operators liable for the same damage can be held jointly and severally liable. Coming soon
Art. 12(2) Limits recourse against defective software component makers in certain circumstances.
Art. 12(2)(a) Applies when the software component maker was a micro or small enterprise. Coming soon
Art. 12(2)(b) Applies when the integrating manufacturer contractually waived its recourse right. Coming soon
Art. 13 Reduction of liability
Art. 13(1) Liability is not reduced because a third party's act also contributed to damage. Coming soon
Art. 13(2) Liability may be reduced when the injured person's own fault contributed. Coming soon
Art. 14 Right of recourseAn operator that paid compensation may seek recovery from other liable operators. Coming soon
Art. 15 Exclusion or limitation of liabilityContract terms or national law cannot limit or exclude liability toward injured persons. Coming soon
Art. 16 Limitation period
Art. 16(1) Three-year limitation period applies, starting when the injured person knew or should have known key facts.
Art. 16(1)(a) Period runs from awareness of the damage. Coming soon
Art. 16(1)(b) Period runs from awareness of the defect. Coming soon
Art. 16(1)(c) Period runs from awareness of which liable operator is responsible. Coming soon
Art. 16(2) National rules on suspending or interrupting limitation periods remain unaffected. Coming soon
Art. 17 Expiry period
Art. 17(1) Compensation claims lapse ten years after the product's market placement unless proceedings were already started.
Art. 17(1)(a) Ten-year period starts when the defective product was first marketed or put into service. Coming soon
Art. 17(1)(b) For substantially modified products, the period starts when the modified version is made available. Coming soon
Art. 17(2) Latent personal injury claims get an extended twenty-five-year limit unless proceedings were already begun. Coming soon
Art. 18 Derogation from development risk defence
Art. 18(1) States may keep existing rules removing the development-risk defence, notifying the Commission by a deadline. Coming soon
Art. 18(2) States may introduce or amend rules denying the development-risk defence for liable operators. Coming soon
Art. 18(3) Introduces conditions that national derogation measures must satisfy.
Art. 18(3)(a) Derogation measures must be restricted to particular product categories. Coming soon
Art. 18(3)(b) Derogation measures must be grounded in public interest aims. Coming soon
Art. 18(3)(c) Derogation measures must be proportionate, suitable and no broader than necessary. Coming soon
Art. 18(4) States must notify the Commission of proposed derogation measures with a justification. Coming soon
Art. 18(5) Commission may opine within six months; the State must hold its measure in abeyance meanwhile. Coming soon
Art. 19 Transparency
Art. 19(1) States must publish final appeal-level judgments under this Directive in accessible electronic form. Coming soon
Art. 19(2) The Commission must maintain a public database of those judgments. Coming soon
Art. 20 EvaluationThe Commission must periodically evaluate the Directive and report to EU institutions. Coming soon
Art. 21 Repeal and transitional provisionThe previous 1985 directive is repealed from December 2026, still applying to earlier-marketed products. Coming soon
Art. 22 Transposition
Art. 22(1) States must adopt implementing national laws by the deadline and refer to this Directive. Coming soon
Art. 22(2) States must send the Commission the main national measures adopted in this field. Coming soon
Art. 23 Entry into forceThe Directive takes effect twenty days after publication in the Official Journal. Coming soon
Art. 24 AddresseesThe Directive is addressed to the Member States. Coming soon
